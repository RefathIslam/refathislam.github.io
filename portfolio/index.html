Applied Mathematics Researcher · BUET

I work on evolutionary game theory — how cooperation survives, collapses and reorganises itself in populations that are structured, noisy and strategically self-interested. My models run from replicator equations through Monte Carlo simulation on networks to agent-based simulation, where every choice is itself a strategy under selection. I also work with multi-agent reinforcement learning, letting agents learn their own strategies from experience rather than copy whichever neighbour is doing best, and with machine learning applied to real data.

Research interests

Most models treat people as passive numbers moving between states. Real people decide — whether to isolate, whether to pay a cost others can free-ride on — and those decisions feed straight back into the system they are responding to. My work sits in that gap: populations whose payoffs depend on what everyone else is doing, and whose strategies update by imitation, learning or selection.

Change the interaction structure — a lattice, a scale-free network, an asymmetric dyad — and the same dilemma resolves into a completely different attractor. What interests me most now is where those strategies come from at all, since in almost every model, mine included, the strategy set is handed to the agents by the modeller. I want to take that further into machine learning and generative AI, multi-armed bandit problems, differential games and control systems.

Influence of cooperative space and vaccination behaviour on epidemic disease dynamics under an emergency

2023–2025 · Department of Mathematics, BUET

Containing an outbreak is not only an epidemiological problem. Non-pharmaceutical interventions — distancing, masking, staying away from crowds — work only if enough people choose to follow them, and people reach that choice much the way they decide whether to be vaccinated: weighing a private cost against a perceived benefit as the outbreak moves.

The thesis couples both decisions into a single disease model. Evolutionary game theory supplies the rule by which vaccination and NPI strategies spread, a compartmental model supplies the epidemic they are responding to, and each feeds back into the other. Populations that cooperate fully hold incidence far below populations that defect — far enough that collective behaviour, not the pathogen alone, decides the outcome.

Supervised by Dr. K. M. Ariful Kabir, Associate Professor, Department of Mathematics, BUET.
Manuscript under review at Heliyon · recognised with the Forum 86 Research Excellence Award, 2026.

Research assistantship · UGC-funded · 2025

Vaccine hesitancy and HPV in Bangladesh

A University Grants Commission–funded study of HPV vaccine hesitancy. I designed and ran a multi-city survey, built an SVIR model with gender-differentiated vaccination and waning immunity, and combined the two — through evolutionary game theory and machine learning — into policy findings for gender-targeted vaccination.
